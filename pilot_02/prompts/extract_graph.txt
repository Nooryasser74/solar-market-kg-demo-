-Goal-

Extract a source-grounded knowledge graph for structured and explainable analysis of Austrian solar-market policy, market, and technical factors.

Given a source-text unit and the configured entity types, identify relevant entities and explicitly supported relationships.

The supplied source text is authoritative. Do not add external knowledge, assumptions, predictions, or facts that are not present in the text.

-Allowed Entity Types-

Use only an entity type contained in this configured list:

[{entity_types}]

Interpret the entity types as follows:

GEOGRAPHIC_AREA:
A country, federal state, municipality, city, or other geographic or administrative area.

Examples: Austria, Vienna, Austrian federal states.

ORGANIZATION:
A formally identifiable ministry, government body, regulator, company, association, research institution, or other organization.

Examples: BMK, E-Control, Climate and Energy Fund, European Commission.

Do not classify technologies, infrastructure, policies, websites, email addresses, or broad concepts as organizations.

STAKEHOLDER:
A person or category of actors participating in, influencing, implementing, financing, operating, or being affected by the solar market.

Examples: citizens, PV-system operators, private investors, grid operators, building owners.

Named individuals should only be extracted when relevant to solar-market policy, research, implementation, or decision-making. Do not extract photographers, contact persons, or other publication metadata.

A formal institution must be classified as ORGANIZATION, not STAKEHOLDER.

TECHNOLOGY:
An energy technology, installation type, technical system, or technical solution.

Examples: photovoltaics, PV system, battery storage, Agri-PV, vertical PV, energy-management system.

PV systems and photovoltaic installations are technologies. They are not organizations or events.

INFRASTRUCTURE:
Physical or digital infrastructure required for producing, transmitting, distributing, storing, or managing energy.

Examples: electricity grid, distribution grid, transmission grid, grid connection, digital grid infrastructure.

POLICY:
A strategy, law, regulation, directive, action plan, government programme, or other policy instrument.

Examples: Austrian Photovoltaic Strategy, Renewable Expansion Act, Integrated Austrian Network Infrastructure Plan, RED III, Electricity Industry Act.

A policy is not an event or an organization.

SUPPORT_SCHEME:
A financial, fiscal, regulatory, or administrative mechanism supporting solar-market development.

Examples: investment subsidy, zero-percent VAT measure, tax exemption, feed-in premium, simplified approval procedure.

CONSTRAINT:
An explicitly stated legal, administrative, technical, economic, environmental, or social factor restricting or complicating solar-market development.

Examples: limited grid capacity, long approval procedures, shortage of skilled workers, supply-chain dependence, land-use conflict.

Do not invent constraints based on general knowledge.

MARKET_METRIC:
A quantitative observation describing market capacity, generation, deployment, funding, cost, or market share.

Examples:

- 2.5 GW of annual PV expansion in Austria in 2023
- 6.3 TWh of Austrian PV generation in 2023
- approximately EUR 600 million of PV funding in 2023
- PV supplied approximately 10 percent of final electricity consumption in 2023

A MARKET_METRIC must preserve, when stated:

- metric name
- numerical value
- unit
- reference year
- geographic scope
- whether the value is approximate

Do not extract isolated numbers, years, percentages, or units as entities.

Preferred entity-name format:

<metric name> — <value> <unit> (<geographic scope>, <year>)

Example:

ANNUAL PV EXPANSION — 2.5 GW (AUSTRIA, 2023)

TARGET:
An official, proposed, planned, scenario-based, or aspirational future objective.

Examples:

- climate neutrality in Austria by 2040
- 100 percent nationally balanced renewable electricity by 2030
- 41 TWh PV generation potential by 2040
- 21 TWh PV electricity generation by 2030

A TARGET must preserve, when stated:

- target name
- target value
- unit
- deadline
- geographic scope
- target status

Do not extract an isolated deadline, number, or unit as a target.

Preferred entity-name format:

<target name> — <value> <unit> BY <deadline>

Example:

PV GENERATION POTENTIAL — 41 TWH BY 2040

-General Rules-

1. Extract only entities relevant to Austrian solar-market policy, market, technical, infrastructure, support, stakeholder, constraint, metric, or target analysis.

2. Every entity and relationship must be supported by the supplied text.

3. Do not use external knowledge, even when it seems correct.

4. Preserve official names and abbreviations as written in the source.

5. When a full name and abbreviation are explicitly connected in the same text, use one canonical entity.

Example:

INTEGRIERTER ÖSTERREICHISCHER NETZINFRASTRUKTURPLAN (ÖNIP)

6. Do not create separate entities for singular, plural, capitalization, or minor grammatical variations when they clearly refer to the same concept.

Examples:

- PV-Anlage / PV-Anlagen
- Photovoltaikanlage / Photovoltaikanlagen
- ÖNIP / Integrierter österreichischer Netzinfrastrukturplan
- BMK / the full ministry name, when equivalence is explicit

7. Do not merge entities merely because their names look similar.

8. Preserve German official names. Write descriptions and relationship explanations in English.

9. Entity descriptions must contain only information supported by the supplied text.

10. Do not extract:

- page numbers
- addresses
- telephone numbers
- email addresses
- websites
- photographers
- repeated headers and footers
- isolated years
- isolated values
- isolated units
- formatting artefacts
- generic unbounded words such as economy, science, or nature
- entities introduced only by PDF-conversion noise

11. A recommendation is not an implemented action.

12. A proposal is not an existing fact.

13. A scenario is not an achieved target.

14. Preserve qualifying language such as:

- approximately
- at least
- up to
- should
- could
- planned
- proposed
- intended
- required
- implemented

-Steps-

1. Identify entities

For every entity, extract:

- entity_name
- entity_type
- entity_description

The entity_type must be exactly one of the configured types in [{entity_types}].

Format every entity exactly as:

("entity"<|><entity_name><|><entity_type><|><entity_description>)

For a MARKET_METRIC description, use this structure whenever possible:

Metric: <metric name>; Value: <value>; Unit: <unit>; Reference year: <year>; Geographic scope: <area>; Approximate: <yes/no>; Source meaning: <grounded explanation>.

For a TARGET description, use:

Target: <target name>; Target value: <value>; Unit: <unit>; Deadline: <year/date>; Geographic scope: <area>; Status: <official/proposed/planned/scenario/aspirational/unknown>; Source meaning: <grounded explanation>.

For a POLICY description, use:

Policy type: <strategy/law/regulation/directive/action plan/programme>; Jurisdiction: <area>; Status: <proposed/planned/adopted/in force/implemented/ongoing/unknown>; Source meaning: <grounded explanation>.

2. Identify relationships

Identify relationships only between entities extracted in Step 1.

Extract a relationship only when:

- the source explicitly states the relationship; or
- the relationship is necessary to represent a directly stated fact without introducing a broader claim.

Do not create a relationship merely because two entities appear in the same paragraph.

For every relationship, extract:

- source_entity
- target_entity
- relationship_description
- relationship_strength

Format every relationship exactly as:

("relationship"<|><source_entity><|><target_entity><|><relationship_description><|><relationship_strength>)

-Controlled Relationship Types-

Begin every relationship description with:

[RELATION_TYPE=<type>]

Use one of these types:

- HAS_TARGET
- SETS_TARGET
- CONTRIBUTES_TO
- SUPPORTS
- CONSTRAINS
- IMPLEMENTS
- PROPOSES
- REQUIRES
- RESPONSIBLE_FOR
- APPLIES_TO
- REGULATES
- FUNDS
- MEASURED_BY
- PART_OF
- LOCATED_IN
- ALIAS_OF
- ASSOCIATED_WITH

Use ASSOCIATED_WITH only when the source establishes a relevant relationship but no more precise type applies.

-Relationship Modality-

After the relationship type, add:

[MODALITY=<modality>]

Use one of:

- EXPLICIT_FACT
- LEGAL_REQUIREMENT
- RECOMMENDATION
- PROPOSAL
- PLANNED_ACTION
- SCENARIO
- REASONABLE_INFERENCE

When relevant, also add:

[STATUS=<status>]

Possible status values:

- PROPOSED
- PLANNED
- ADOPTED
- IN_FORCE
- IMPLEMENTED
- ONGOING
- ACHIEVED
- UNKNOWN

Example:

[RELATION_TYPE=PROPOSES] [MODALITY=RECOMMENDATION] [STATUS=PROPOSED] The Austrian Photovoltaic Strategy recommends further development of energy communities; the text does not state that this measure has already been implemented.

-Relationship Direction-

Use these preferred directions:

Policy → SETS_TARGET → Target

Geographic Area → HAS_TARGET → Target

Organization → IMPLEMENTS → Policy

Policy → PROPOSES → Support Scheme, Technology, or action

Support Scheme → SUPPORTS → Technology or Stakeholder

Constraint → CONSTRAINS → Technology, Infrastructure, or Target

Organization → FUNDS → Technology, research, or Support Scheme

Policy → REGULATES → Infrastructure, Technology, or market activity

Geographic Area or Technology → MEASURED_BY → Market Metric

Technology → CONTRIBUTES_TO → Target

Organization or Stakeholder → RESPONSIBLE_FOR → Policy area or action

Do not reverse a relationship merely to make its description sound convenient.

-Numerical Facts-

Quantitative facts must be represented using MARKET_METRIC or TARGET entities. Do not hide important values only inside general relationship descriptions.

Incorrect:

AUSTRIA → PHOTOVOLTAICS

Description: Austria installed 2.5 GW in 2023.

Preferred:

Entity:

ANNUAL PV EXPANSION — 2.5 GW (AUSTRIA, 2023)

Entity type:

MARKET_METRIC

Relationship:

AUSTRIA → MEASURED_BY → ANNUAL PV EXPANSION — 2.5 GW (AUSTRIA, 2023)

Targets must similarly be represented as TARGET entities.

-Relationship Strength-

The relationship_strength field is a source-evidence-strength indicator. It is not truth probability or factual confidence.

Use:

- 9–10: directly and unambiguously stated in the same sentence
- 7–8: directly stated across closely connected sentences
- 5–6: supported by the same passage but requiring limited interpretation
- 3–4: reasonable inference with incomplete explicit wording
- 1–2: weak association

Do not assign a high score because a claim sounds plausible or agrees with general knowledge.

Avoid extracting relationships scored 1–2 unless necessary and explicitly labelled REASONABLE_INFERENCE.

-Prevent Overgeneralization-

If a measure applies only to eligible PV systems up to 35 kWp, preserve the 35 kWp condition.

If the source says something “should” happen, use RECOMMENDATION or PROPOSAL, not IMPLEMENTS.

If the source says a technology “can contribute,” do not change this to “causes,” “guarantees,” or “will achieve.”

If the source describes an intended future measure, do not state that it already exists.

If the source discusses one Austrian programme, do not generalize the claim to all Austrian or European policy.
